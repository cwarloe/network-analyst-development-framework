# Prompt — R009, reviewer agreement without a rubric

**Issued:** 2026-08-19 · **For:** an external research model · **Answers into:** R009

Framed because [R006](../R006-literature-scan.md) established that trained raters with detailed analytic rubrics reach weighted kappa around 0.79–0.83, and this project's [capstone review guide](../../../lessons/09-capstone-encrypted-outbound-traffic/review-guide.md) is deliberately **not** a rubric. The planned paired-review trial will therefore produce low verdict agreement, and without knowing what that means in advance the result will be misread.

Kept because [the protocol](../PROTOCOL.md) says to record the question as framed.

---

```
You are researching an assessment methodology question. I need a
decision-ready brief, not an essay.

## The situation

I have a curriculum whose assessments are open-ended written work
products — an analyst reads evidence and produces an assessment, a
diagnosis, or a handoff document. There are no scores, levels, or
mastery thresholds, deliberately: nothing in the project is validated
well enough to grade anyone, and adding a number would imply otherwise.

Review is guided by a set of things a reviewer should look for — whether
the writer separated observation from interpretation, whether they argued
the benign explanation seriously, whether they stated what they could not
determine — but it is explicitly NOT a rubric. No criteria, no scale, no
scoring.

I want to run a check: two reviewers independently review the same work
product, then compare. The question is what that check can actually tell
me.

## The question

What can be learned from inter-reviewer comparison of open-ended
professional work when there is no rubric — and what cannot?

Specifically:

1. What agreement is realistically achievable without a rubric? Published
   figures for rubric-based performance assessment cluster around
   weighted kappa 0.79–0.83 with trained raters. What happens without one?

2. Is verdict agreement the right measure at all? My instinct is that the
   useful question is whether two reviewers NOTICE the same things — flag
   the same weak claim, the same missing alternative — rather than whether
   they reach the same overall judgment. Is there methodological support
   for that distinction, and does it have a name?

3. How is agreement measured for "what did they notice"? If the unit is
   a flagged issue rather than a score, what statistics apply? How are
   partial overlaps and differently-worded observations about the same
   underlying problem handled?

4. What sample size makes this informative? Two reviewers on one work
   product tells me something. What is the smallest design that supports
   a defensible conclusion?

5. When is low agreement a finding about the instrument versus about the
   work versus about the reviewers? All three are possible and I need to
   know how to tell them apart.

6. What does the literature say about formative assessment reliability
   thresholds? I have seen claims that lower reliability is acceptable for
   open-ended formative work. Is that a real methodological position with
   support, or a convenience?

## Output

- **Part 1:** What inter-reviewer comparison can establish without a
  rubric, and what it cannot.
- **Part 2:** The measure to use, with its statistic, and why.
- **Part 3:** A concrete minimum design — how many reviewers, how many
  work products, what to record.
- **Part 4:** How to interpret the result, including how to distinguish
  the three causes of low agreement.
- **Part 5:** What you could not determine.

## Evidence standards

- Cite sources and give figures where they exist.
- Distinguish established methodology from reasonable-sounding practice.
- If the honest answer to question 1 is "nobody has studied this because
  nobody assesses without rubrics," say so — that is a finding.
- Do not propose adding a rubric. The absence of one is a deliberate
  design decision and the question is what can be learned within it.
```
