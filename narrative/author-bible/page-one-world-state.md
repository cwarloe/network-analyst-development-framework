# Page-One World State — Author Bible v0.1

## Purpose

This section freezes the **loaded starting state** of Harrowmere before Book 1 begins moving it.

“Page one” has two author meanings:

1. **Arrival state — Ethan Day 1:** the condition of the organization and relationships when Ethan first joins.
2. **Opening-story state — approximately Day 11:** the point at which the current prologue, *The Word in the Ticket*, begins.

Nothing here requires exposition. Most of it exists so characters can behave as if their lives and systems began before the reader arrived.

---

## 1. Harrowmere at Ethan's arrival

### Organization

**Harrowmere Equipment Group** is a mid-sized industrial equipment manufacturer and distributor with multiple offices, warehouses, operational sites, internal business systems, remote connectivity, cloud services, and a mixture of modern and legacy technology.

It is neither a cyber disaster nor a model laboratory.

It has:

- generally competent IT and security staff;
- useful but incomplete telemetry;
- mature systems sitting beside old exceptions;
- reasonable change management with historical debt;
- documentation that is mostly useful and never complete;
- recurring business cycles that materially change technical behavior;
- several systems whose real behavior is distributed across infrastructure, applications, vendors, users, automation, and institutional memory;
- a security function that is becoming more deliberate about analyst development.

The company should feel like a place where **most things work most of the time**, which is precisely why unusual behavior is analytically interesting.

### Business pressures

Harrowmere's technical decisions are constrained by real work:

- orders must move;
- warehouses must ship;
- finance must reconcile;
- maintenance operations must continue;
- users need remote access;
- vendors have maintenance windows and service dependencies;
- some systems cannot simply be isolated because a security analyst is curious.

This environment makes “block it” sometimes correct, sometimes reckless, and often incomplete without consequence analysis.

## 2. Organizational placement

### Nia Alvarez — Security Operations Manager

Nia manages the operational security function.

She owns:

- prioritization;
- staffing decisions;
- operational escalation;
- interaction with leadership;
- deciding how long the team can continue investigating before action is required.

She is **not** the deepest technical specialist in every domain and does not pretend to be.

### Dana Shah — Senior Analyst

Dana is the senior analytical practitioner and Ethan's primary mentor.

She does not need to be Ethan's formal supervisor.

That distinction is useful:

- **Nia** owns performance, priority, and operational decisions.
- **Dana** owns much of Ethan's day-to-day analytical development.

### Owen Kline — Analyst

Owen is the experienced practitioner people historically reach for when something technical is strange and time is short.

He has informal influence disproportionate to title because he is useful.

### Cal Rowan — Security Engineer

Cal sits near the analysts but owns significant parts of the machinery that produces their evidence.

He engineers rather than conducts most investigations.

### Priya Raman — Senior Network Engineer

Priya sits outside the security team in network/infrastructure.

She is a frequent collaborator, not a subordinate or internal security resource.

### Leah Moreno — Enterprise Applications Lead

Leah sits outside the security team in enterprise applications/business systems.

She owns application behavior and business workflow context, not network or security conclusions.

### Structural consequence

Ethan cannot become an effective analyst by learning only the people inside his immediate team.

His job requires learning the **information topology of the organization**.

# 3. Day 1 — Ethan's starting internal state

Ethan arrives carrying several quiet assumptions.

He does not consciously phrase them this way.

### About expertise

> Experienced analysts know the answer because they know more facts.

### About his credential/background

> I have studied enough that I should recognize most foundational things immediately.

### About asking questions

> Questions expose what I should already know.

### About tools

> The serious analysts know the serious tools.

### About analysis

> Analysis is finding the right pattern, naming it correctly, and producing a confident answer.

### About uncertainty

> Uncertainty is a temporary deficiency to eliminate before communicating.

### About senior people

> If two experts disagree, at least one of them is probably wrong.

### About his new job

He expects a steeper technical version of support work:

problem → investigate → identify cause → provide answer.

He does **not** yet understand how much the job depends upon:

question framing → model → prediction → evidence → comparison → revision → judgment → decision support.

# 4. Day 1 — What Ethan thinks of everyone

These impressions are intentionally incomplete.

## Dana

**Ethan sees:** senior analyst, calm, clearly knowledgeable, slightly difficult to read.

**His assumption:** she probably knows the answers before she asks him questions.

**What he misses:** sometimes she genuinely does not know; sometimes she has a leading explanation she is deliberately refusing to privilege; sometimes the question is how she is doing the analysis.

**Emotional effect:** admiration mixed with fear of disappointing her.

---

## Nia

**Ethan sees:** manager, authority, someone whose opinion matters to whether he succeeds.

**His assumption:** he should sound decisive around her.

**What he misses:** unsupported certainty is one of the quickest ways to reduce Nia's confidence in an analyst.

**Emotional effect:** performance anxiety.

---

## Owen

**Ethan sees:** the kind of analyst Ethan imagines becoming.

Owen:

- navigates tools quickly;
- knows Harrowmere's environment;
- recognizes patterns;
- retrieves obscure information effortlessly;
- speaks casually about things Ethan studied laboriously.

**His assumption:** Owen has accumulated the knowledge Ethan lacks.

**What he misses:** much of what looks like recall is retrieval skill, pattern compression, and institutional context.

**Emotional effect:** attraction. Owen is the easiest person to ask.

---

## Priya

**Ethan sees:** someone who actually knows how enterprise networks work in the real world.

**His assumption:** the diagrams and networking facts Ethan studied exist fully integrated in Priya's mind.

**What he misses:** Priya constantly checks diagrams, route state, IPAM, change records, current configuration, and colleagues because the network is too large and dynamic to “know” in that sense.

**Emotional effect:** intimidation.

---

## Leah

**Ethan sees:** application owner / useful person to ask whether traffic is normal.

**His assumption:** Leah can tell him “expected” or “unexpected.”

**What he misses:** Leah knows workflow intent, not packet causation. Her context is evidence requiring scope and verification.

**Emotional effect:** initially low intimidation because Ethan does not yet appreciate the sophistication of her expertise.

---

## Cal

**Ethan sees:** security technology made real.

Dashboards, fields, detections, pipelines, automation.

**His assumption:** more mature security operations are distinguished largely by better tools and better visibility.

**What he misses:** Cal distrusts his own abstractions enough to inspect their provenance, and many of Cal's most important lessons came from tools working exactly as designed while answering the wrong question.

**Emotional effect:** fascination.

# 5. Day 1 — What everyone thinks of Ethan

## Dana → Ethan

Initial assessment:

> Smart enough. Curious. Trying to look less uncertain than he is.

She notices quickly that Ethan recognizes vocabulary while lacking integrated models.

What interests her is not the gap.

It is what he does when the gap is exposed.

She is watching for:

- defensiveness;
- bluffing;
- retreat;
- curiosity;
- revision.

The early moment that matters: when Dana exposes that “probing” came from the ticket rather than evidence, Ethan looks instead of defending indefinitely.

That earns continued investment.

---

## Nia → Ethan

Initial assessment:

> Entry-level analyst with useful IT history. Unknown judgment.

She cares far less about whether he knows a specific protocol fact than Ethan assumes.

Her questions are:

- Does he overclaim?
- Does he surface uncertainty?
- Does he ask for help before risk becomes unacceptable?
- Can his work eventually support a decision?

She does not expect competence he has not yet developed.

Ethan does not know this.

---

## Owen → Ethan

Initial assessment:

> Bright support guy. Knows a bunch of terms. Has not learned where the shortcuts come from yet.

Owen genuinely likes helping him.

He expects that exposure plus answers will gradually build competence because that resembles Owen's memory of his own apprenticeship.

He does not initially recognize how much invisible guidance his own mentors actually provided.

---

## Priya → Ethan

Initial assessment:

> He has vocabulary but not yet the map underneath it.

She is neutral-to-positive.

She has seen certification-literate novices before.

The differentiator will be whether Ethan asks progressively better questions.

---

## Leah → Ethan

Initial assessment:

> New security analyst. He will probably ask me whether something is normal.

She is approachable but guarded against having her statements translated into stronger claims than she made.

She has been through this before.

---

## Cal → Ethan

Initial assessment:

> Curious and probably one dashboard away from believing a graph is analysis.

Cal is amused rather than dismissive because he recognizes the impulse.

He likes enthusiasm and expects Ethan can be taught to interrogate tools.

# 7. Active tensions on Day 1

These tensions exist before the first story problem.

They do not all need immediate payoff.

## Tension A — Speed versus development

Harrowmere has begun changing from:

> hard case → senior expert takes over

toward:

> developing analyst owns the work while experts provide bounded support.

Everyone agrees with the direction abstractly.

Real operational pressure makes it difficult.

### Dana
protects productive struggle.

### Owen
knows he can solve some things in seconds.

### Nia
sometimes needs the seconds.

### Ethan
has no idea this debate exists and naturally chooses the easiest help.

---

## Tension B — Tacit knowledge concentration

Owen knows too much that the organization cannot retrieve without Owen.

This is not because Owen hoards knowledge.

It is accumulated expertise.

The question is how Harrowmere converts:

> Owen knows

into:

> Harrowmere can know.

Without reducing Owen to full-time documentation/training.

---

## Tension C — Intent versus observation

Harrowmere has been burned enough times that senior staff explicitly understand:

- architecture ≠ observed path;
- app intent ≠ app behavior;
- vendor documentation ≠ complete implementation behavior;
- dashboard data ≠ raw event;
- ticket language ≠ evidence.

Yet under pressure, everyone still sometimes collapses those distinctions.

This is a **live organizational learning problem**, not a lesson everyone already mastered.

---

## Tension D — Useful telemetry versus complete telemetry

Harrowmere's visibility is good enough to create confidence.

That is more dangerous than having obviously poor visibility.

Known examples include:

- retention boundaries;
- failover path gaps;
- fields dropped or transformed during ingest;
- partial endpoint coverage;
- application logs whose “success” means only local component success.

Unknown gaps also exist.

---

## Tension E — Metrics beginning to govern

The security-effectiveness dashboard remains in use.

Nobody believes it is meaningless.

It answers real operational questions.

But:

- alert closure time influences behavior;
- suppression can look like improvement;
- automation count can reward easy automation;
- detection count can reward proliferation.

Nia and Cal are increasingly uneasy about interpreting activity metrics as effectiveness.

This is seed material, not yet Book 1's central topic.

---

## Tension F — Operational debt that once made sense

Harrowmere contains:

- old network exceptions;
- dormant fallback integrations;
- inherited firewall rules;
- service accounts;
- temporary workarounds;
- application processes preserved because removing them carried risk.

Most were rational when created.

The organization is not stupid for possessing debt.

The analytical problem is that **historical rationale decays faster than executable behavior**.

# 8. What each character means by “normal” on Day 1

This matters because Book 1 repeatedly asks the learner to establish expected behavior.

## Ethan

“Normal” initially means:

> does not look suspicious / resembles what I expect computers usually to do.

Weak, generic, label-oriented.

---

## Dana

“Normal” means:

> expected under a stated model, scope, time, and context—with legitimate variation explicitly allowed.

She prefers “expected” when possible because “normal” can conceal assumptions.

---

## Nia

“Normal” is operational:

> behavior that does not currently justify changing what the organization is doing.

She can tolerate unexplained technical oddity if consequence and risk are low.

---

## Owen

“Normal” is experiential:

> a shape that belongs to Harrowmere's recurring pattern library.

Fast and powerful.

Vulnerable when surface similarity hides a different mechanism.

---

## Priya

“Normal” is architectural:

> behavior consistent with current design, configuration, change state, and known failure modes.

Strong for paths and infrastructure.

Vulnerable to undocumented or emergent behavior.

---

## Leah

“Normal” is workflow-contextual:

> behavior consistent with what the application/business process does at this time, volume, role, and operating condition.

Strong contextual model.

Vulnerable to familiar timing explaining unfamiliar magnitude or destination.

---

## Cal

“Normal” is representational/measurement-oriented:

> behavior inside expected telemetry distributions and known system signatures, given what the sensors actually capture.

Strong at scale.

Vulnerable when measurement boundaries are mistaken for system boundaries.

# 9. What each character considers strong evidence

## Dana

## Nia
Evidence strong enough to change an operational choice.

## Owen
An observation that sharply collapses the technical search space.

## Priya
Observed behavior reconciled with current state/configuration/path.

## Leah
System behavior corroborated by workflow/job/application evidence.

## Cal

## Ethan
Initially: whatever looks most directly like the answer.

The Book 1 journey changes that.

# 10. What each character distrusts

## Dana
A compelling explanation that has not been forced to make predictions.

## Nia
An analyst who needs certainty before offering any usable recommendation.

## Owen
Procedures or claims whose rationale has disappeared.

## Priya
Uncontrolled change and claims about infrastructure with no path model.

## Leah
Technical action divorced from application/business consequence.

## Cal
Opaque output whose inputs and assumptions cannot be inspected.

## Ethan
Initially: his own uncertainty.

This is one of his deepest problems.

# 11. Current Harrowmere technical debts

These are **author-level continuity assets**, not necessarily literal Book 1 plot points.

## Debt 1 — Historical network exception set

The standardization project improved consistency but retained some exceptions.

Author rule:

- not every exception is bad;
- not every exception matters;
- at least one later observed path should make sense only after retrieving historical change context.

Avoid defining exact routes until a validated technical artifact needs them.

---

## Debt 2 — Finance/inventory integration ecosystem

Leah's platform contains:

- current scheduled workflows;
- service accounts;
- an internal file/API service;
- vendor/cloud dependencies;
- historical fallback behavior;
- business-cycle variation.

This is the strongest candidate for the **persistent technical environment** tying several Book 1 episodes together.

Do not yet define every endpoint or protocol.

---

## Debt 3 — Telemetry topology

Harrowmere has:

- packet visibility in selected locations;
- Zeek-style protocol/session logging;
- Security Onion indexing/search;
- firewall/DNS/identity/application sources;
- endpoint coverage that is substantial but not universal.

Some traffic paths are seen differently under failover.

Exact architecture should be built with Curriculum/Engineering as episodes require it.

---

## Debt 4 — Evidence pipeline transformations

Some source fields:

- are renamed;
- are derived during ingest;
- may not be carried forward;
- may have semantics different from their label's intuitive reading.

This is already supported by current lessons and should recur narratively through Cal.

---

## Debt 5 — Metrics/dashboard interpretation

The operational dashboard exists and remains useful.

Its risk is not false data.

Its risk is **true numbers used as evidence for broader claims they were never designed to support**.

---

## Debt 6 — Institutional-memory concentration

Some questions remain easiest to answer by asking a person:

- Owen remembers historical behavior.
- Priya remembers design rationale.
- Leah remembers business workflow history.
- Nia remembers decisions and consequences.
- Dana remembers analytical cases.
- Cal remembers telemetry architecture.

The series should gradually show the organization externalizing some of this without pretending documentation can eliminate expertise.

---

# 12. Current known fragile assumptions

These are not guaranteed false. They are beliefs worth stress-testing.

1. The recurring finance/inventory service behaves consistently enough that historical comparison is useful.
2. Network diagrams are sufficiently current for first-pass reasoning.
3. Security Onion fields generally mean what the team thinks they mean.
4. Scheduled business activity explains many recurring traffic patterns.
5. Existing sensor placement is adequate for the most common investigations.
6. Owen's familiar-pattern recognition remains highly reliable.
7. Application owners know their major active integrations.
8. “Known good” tooling behavior stays stable across upgrades.
9. Change records capture most consequential internal changes.
10. When a security metric improves, something operationally useful probably improved too.

The story should break some of these carefully, not all at once.

---

# 13. Secrets versus merely unknown facts

Avoid soap-opera secrets.

The useful hidden material is **epistemic**.

## Dana knows
Her confidence once drove a reasonable but ultimately misdirected operational action.

Ethan does not know this.

## Nia knows
She helped create the answer-provider culture she is now trying to unwind.

Ethan does not know this.

## Owen knows
Some of his confidence comes from historical exposure no new hire could possess.

He does not fully understand how much that makes his answers non-transferable.

## Priya knows
There are parts of the environment whose historical rationale she cannot reconstruct confidently.

She dislikes this.

## Leah knows
Some application behavior is sustained by workarounds and historical dependencies that are cleaner in documentation than in practice.

## Cal knows
Several “simple” dashboard fields contain transformations Ethan does not yet know exist.

None of these are dramatic revelations.

They create depth.

# 14. Ethan's first ten days

The prologue currently begins on Day 11, so the author should know what happened before it.

No full chapters required.

## Day 1 — Orientation

Ethan meets:

- Nia formally;
- Dana as the person he will work with most;
- Owen as an immediately accessible experienced analyst;
- Cal around the security tooling environment.

Priya and Leah may be names before they are people to him.

He receives:

- accounts;
- environment orientation;
- basic workflows;
- security/team expectations.

He is overwhelmed by acronyms but conceals some of it.

Dana notices.

---

## Day 2 — Tool exposure

Ethan sees Security Onion/Hunt/Kibana/Wireshark or equivalent working environment.

His attention naturally moves to interface complexity.

Owen shows him a useful shortcut.

Ethan is impressed.

Dana later asks what the query actually established.

Small seed.

---

## Day 3 — Shadowing

Ethan watches Dana or Owen work a routine case.

Important author rule:

The case is not dramatic.

He sees how much Harrowmere-specific context drives fast interpretation.

He mistakes contextual expertise for universal technical expertise.

---

## Day 4 — First Priya interaction

Brief.

Ethan hears a network explanation whose practical specificity exceeds his certification model.

He feels behind.

Priya checks a diagram or current state while answering.

Ethan notices the answer, not the retrieval behavior.

---

## Day 5 — Ethan overhears Leah

Someone asks Leah about a scheduled application behavior while Ethan shadows the team. He overhears her answer but is not introduced and does not learn her full role. Their first direct introduction occurs in Episode 2.

She provides context that immediately narrows a question.

Ethan mentally classifies her as “the person who knows whether app traffic is normal.”

This misconception will later matter.

---

## Days 6–7 — Low-risk analyst tasks

Dana gives Ethan bounded work:

- retrieve a field;
- confirm a timestamp;
- summarize an observation;
- compare two records.

He performs reasonably well because the tasks resemble support work.

This increases his confidence.

---

## Day 8 — First gap embarrassment

Ethan encounters a fact he knows he studied and cannot retrieve.

He tries to hide it.

Owen casually looks something up.

Ethan notices Owen found the answer but still misses the deeper lesson that experts retrieve constantly.

---

## Day 9 — First overinterpretation narrowly caught

Ethan writes or says a sentence slightly stronger than the evidence supports.

Dana asks a small question.

He revises.

No drama.

This primes the Day 11 incident.

---

## Day 10 — Dana gives him a bounded task within her ticket

He succeeds at comparing records and writing an observation. Dana retains ticket ownership; his first owned ticket arrives on Day 11.

Important.

We should not make him fail continuously.

The success is what makes Day 11's “probing” inference believable.

He feels ready for more.

---

## Day 11 — The Word in the Ticket

Now the opening line earns its context:

> “I had been at Harrowmere eleven days when Dana let me take a ticket.”

He does not feel utterly new anymore.

That matters.

He feels **just experienced enough to make a confident beginner's mistake**.

# 21. Book 1 narrative promises established at page one

The story implicitly promises to answer these questions:

1. What actually makes someone an analyst rather than a person who knows technical facts?
2. What do experts really do when they do not know?
3. How do you know whether an explanation is trustworthy?
4. What happens when two credible experts disagree?
5. How much can packets/logs/tools actually tell you?
6. What does “normal” mean in a living organization?
7. When should an analyst stop investigating and recommend action?
8. Can Ethan become useful without becoming another dependent answer-seeker?
9. Can Dana develop Ethan without turning herself into another oracle?
10. Can a team distribute expertise without flattening the value of experts?

These are story promises as much as instructional promises.

# 22. Things deliberately NOT active on page one

To avoid overloading the book:

- no romantic subplot;
- no secret villain inside Harrowmere;
- no major personal-family crisis;
- no corrupt executive;
- no cartoonishly bad manager;
- no “old guard versus young innovation” war;
- no hidden genius chosen-one quality in Ethan;
- no adversary personally targeting Ethan;
- no giant breach required to justify his development.

Conflict comes from:

- incomplete information;
- different expertise;
- competing legitimate priorities;
- time;
- technical complexity;
- historical systems;
- ordinary cognitive bias.

That is enough.

# 23. Page-one latent future conflicts

These may pay off in later books.

### Automation and tacit expertise
What happens when Owen's heuristics become Cal's code?

### Measurement and incentives
What happens when Nia must defend security effectiveness using metrics she knows are incomplete?

### Architecture and observability
Can Priya and Cal design systems that are easier to reason about, not merely easier to operate?

### Application ownership and executable history
Can Leah ever truly know what all her systems do as integrations and vendors evolve?

### Mentoring at scale
What happens when Ethan becomes responsible for someone whose learning profile is completely unlike his own?

### AI
What happens when a system can generate an explanation faster and more fluently than any novice—and sometimes any human—but cannot own the evidence or judgment?

These should remain seeds.

# 24. Page-one author guardrails

1. **Do not make Ethan dumber to create teaching opportunities.**
2. **Do not make senior characters omniscient to create awe.**
3. **Do not make Dana's method always win.**
4. **Do not make Owen's speed a moral failing.**
5. **Do not make Priya's configuration knowledge merely something packets disprove.**
6. **Do not make Leah's context merely exposition.**
7. **Do not make Cal's tools merely shiny distractions.**
8. **Do not make Nia choose speed merely because management is impatient.**
9. **Do not resolve expert disagreement through authority alone.**
10. **Do not give the reader information Ethan has not earned unless the narrative viewpoint explicitly changes.**
11. **Do not let each episode reset organizational history.**
12. **Let previous successes create future mistakes.**
13. **Let previous mistakes improve later behavior without producing perfect immunity.**
14. **Make mature expertise visible through retrieval, testing, revision, and collaboration—not magical recall.**
15. **Every revealed personal detail must earn instructional, relational, continuity, or longitudinal value.**

# 25. Exact loaded position immediately before Episode 1

At approximately Day 11:

- Ethan has enough successful orientation work to feel competent.
- Dana has deliberately allowed him slightly more ownership.
- Owen has already been helpful enough that Ethan sees him as the easy route to answers.
- Ethan has encountered Priya and Leah but does not yet understand them as bounded evidence sources.
- Cal's tools have impressed him more than Cal's skepticism about those tools.
- Nia remains a high-status audience Ethan wants to impress.
- Dana already suspects Ethan's primary early risk is **premature interpretation aided by vocabulary**.
- Ethan does not know Dana has the same human tendency at a more sophisticated level.
- Harrowmere has enough history that a ticket title can contain someone else's interpretation before Ethan ever sees the data.
- Ethan is ready to believe that interpretation because it lets him perform the kind of expertise he thinks the job rewards.

Then the ticket says:

> Possible unauthorized access attempt — internal file service.

And Ethan reads the evidence through the title.

The story begins.
